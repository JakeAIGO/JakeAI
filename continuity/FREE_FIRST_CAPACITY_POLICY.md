# JakeAI Free-First Capacity Policy

## Rule

JakeAI uses every legitimate free resource efficiently, circumvents nothing, owns its state and commercial infrastructure, and pays for capacity only when demonstrated demand makes the expenditure rational and the owner explicitly approves it.

## Order of operations

1. Optimize consumption before adding capacity.
2. Use legitimate free tiers, promotional credits, open-source software, and public interfaces only within their published terms.
3. Keep JakeAI state and business logic provider-neutral so a provider can be replaced without reconstructing the project.
4. When one provider is unavailable or legitimately exhausted, preserve state and route eligible work to another permitted provider/capacity source.
5. Never evade quotas, rate limits, access controls, account restrictions, or payment requirements. No fake accounts, identity rotation, credential sharing/theft, unauthorized scraping, or other circumvention.
6. Measure actual consumption and capacity pressure before proposing paid expansion.
7. Paid capacity requires demonstrated need plus explicit owner approval.

## Capacity signals to track

- AI/model availability and legitimate usage exhaustion.
- Context/handoff size and compression efficiency.
- API request volume and rate-limit pressure.
- Website bandwidth and request volume.
- Storage/database growth.
- Build/deployment minutes and failures.
- Background automation volume.
- Social publishing workload.

## Escalation ladder

`optimize -> legitimate free capacity -> alternate permitted free provider -> practical open-source/local capacity -> paid capacity with approval`

A lower rung should be exhausted or demonstrably unsuitable before moving upward.

## Commercial trigger

Do not purchase infrastructure for hypothetical scale. Preserve an architecture capable of scaling, but let verified demand, reliability requirements, or revenue justify actual capacity purchases.

The near-term commercial priority remains JakeAI's first real independent paid sale.
